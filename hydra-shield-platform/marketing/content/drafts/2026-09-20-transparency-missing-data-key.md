Transparency series #1: Why we show the missing data key instead of
working around it.

Every platform in this space faces the same temptation: a location where
one dataset has no coverage. The easy paths are interpolation, a proxy
from a neighbouring cell, or quietly dropping the hazard from the output.

We take the uncomfortable one: the report says "unavailable" — on the
page, next to the hazards that did have data.

The reason is not purity. It is that the person reading the report — an
underwriter, a credit officer, a municipal planner — makes a real
decision from it. A gap they can see is information. A gap that was
silently filled is a hidden assumption in someone else's decision.

So every Talaix output distinguishes three states: evidence found (with
its source), evidence sought but unavailable (stated), and evidence
outside the method's validity (also stated). Nothing in between.
