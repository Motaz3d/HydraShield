How does a DNSH screening report actually inform a credit decision?

"Do No Significant Harm" sounds abstract until a credit committee asks a
concrete question: does financing this asset, in this place, hold up
against the climate adaptation criterion?

That question cannot be answered with a portfolio average. It needs
per-asset evidence:

- Which physical hazards affect this exact location — wildfire, flood,
  drought, heat, wind, coastal — each with its source dataset, period
  and method named on the page
- Which claims are OBSERVED, which are HISTORICAL, which are MODELLED
  or PROJECTED — because a supervisor will ask
- Where the evidence ends — limitations stated in the same document, so
  the credit file shows the boundary instead of discovering it later

What the report deliberately does not contain: invented valuations,
black-box scores, or a precision the data cannot support.
