A grid connection, a port, a data center, a logistics hub — every piece
of infrastructure sits inside hazard layers whether its operators read
them or not.

What "reading the layers" actually means on the Talaix map:

- Each layer is one hazard from one named source — fire detections from
  NASA FIRMS, discharge from GloFAS, heat percentiles against the
  1991–2020 ERA5 baseline
- Layers carry their provenance with them: sensor, period, resolution,
  license. Nothing arrives as an unlabelled blend
- Historical events are drawn from documented archives — so "what
  happened near this asset before" is a query with sources, not a
  search-engine session
- Where coverage ends, the layer ends. The gap is visible on the map,
  not smoothed over

For asset and risk managers, this turns a vague question — "how exposed
are we?" — into a per-site file with evidence attached.
