How the reports were used in real decisions

Three real engagements in which a Talaix report fed a formal decision. Names and identifying details are withheld at the customers' request; every capability described below is a documented output of the reports above.

Credit decision — secured lending

Anonymized · European bank

The credit team of a regional bank was reviewing a secured loan to a company whose collateral included physical production sites. The Green Finance Verification Report was attached to the credit file as the physical climate-risk evidence annex.

  • The DNSH hazard checklist (EU Taxonomy Appendix A vocabulary) gave the credit committee one claim status per hazard — DOCUMENTED, MODELLED or UNKNOWN.
  • Hazards marked UNKNOWN were not assumed away: they became explicit follow-up conditions, and the borrower was asked for additional site documentation before disbursement.
  • The committee minuted that the report is a screening-level evidence layer, per its own disclaimer — not investment advice.

Insurance decision — underwriting referral

Anonymized · (Re)insurance underwriter

An underwriter screening a commercial property risk used the Insurance Environmental Risk Profile as the pre-catastrophe-model evidence view before deciding whether to accept, refer or decline.

  • Per-peril levels and the declared event-history coverage showed which perils rested on observed records and which were declared gaps.
  • The actuarial block — annual event frequency with its exact confidence interval, annual exceedance probability and return period — fed the referral discussion; the composite insurability band routed the file to senior review.
  • The loss-not-quantified rule was respected: the profile informed the accept/refer decision, never the price.

Disclosure decision — CSRD / ESRS E1

Anonymized · Mid-cap company

A sustainability team preparing its first CSRD disclosure used the Sustainability Evidence Report to decide what it could — and could not — state about physical climate risk across its sites.

  • The coverage map (covered_by_evidence, partial, not_covered) set the disclosure boundary for ESRS E1 instead of an unevidenced narrative.
  • Per-site hazard checklists and declared boundaries were handed to the assurance provider as the evidence annex behind the disclosure.
  • Company-declared fields stayed labelled as company-declared — the auditor could see exactly what the engine evidenced and what it did not.

Every report carries its metadata

The first page of each report states its identity and evidence standing, so a report can always be checked against the data it claims to describe:

Report IDA stable content hash of the underlying analysis payload — the same bytes produce the same ID, so a report and its evidence can be verified to match.
GeneratedThe analysis timestamp (real data-acquisition time).
Report engineReport-engine version and risk-model version, with the report type.
Data sourcesThe sources that actually contributed, from the analysis provenance block.
Evidence statusWhich components are observed, derived, modelled or unavailable.
Validation statusWhether the screening score is a validated predictor — currently stated honestly on every report (screening indicator until the validation pipeline passes on real historical data).
