Key terms in plain language

Observed vs modelled
Observed = measured (satellite scene, recorded event). Modelled = computed from a model (forecast, projection, scenario). The filter on the left separates the two — they are never mixed silently.
FWI (Fire Weather Index)
A weather-driven index of how easily fire could spread today. It says nothing about ignition or probability of loss.
NDMI / NDVI
Satellite vegetation signals from Sentinel-2. NDMI ≈ fuel moisture (dry vs moist), NDVI ≈ greenness. A 10 m pixel can see a park, not a single tree.
FIRMS
NASA's near-real-time active-fire detections. A hotspot is a heat detection, not a fire perimeter.
ESA WorldCover
A global 10 m land-cover map used in Map Check to cross-verify what open maps claim.

Map Check

Cross-check what open maps say against what satellite observation shows — and when they disagree, see the most likely reasons why. A discrepancy is a signal to verify, never proof that a map is wrong.

Honesty contract: only open sources are compared (OpenStreetMap, ESA WorldCover, Sentinel-2). Proprietary maps are not fetched.

Why discrepancies happen

Outdated map data

Time lag

OpenStreetMap is edited by volunteers. A park, plantation or land-use change may have happened since the last edit.

Unmapped features

Completeness

Many green spaces are simply not mapped yet — especially private gardens, informal green space and recent planting.

Season & clouds

Observation window

NDVI reflects the most recent cloud-free Sentinel-2 scene. Winter, harvest, drought or clouds can make vegetation look temporarily sparse.

Resolution mismatch

Scale

WorldCover pixels are 10 m and Sentinel-2 pixels are 10–30 m. Small mapped features or narrow tree lines may fall below the pixel size.

Real change

Change

Construction, logging, planting, flooding or fire can change vegetation between the map edit date and the satellite observation.
